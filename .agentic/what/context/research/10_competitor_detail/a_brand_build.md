### Jasper (https://www.jasper.ai)
- **Category:** Marketing AI platform with a brand knowledge layer (Jasper IQ: Brand Voice, Knowledge, Audiences, Style Guide, Visual Guidelines; marketed as Brand IQ + Marketing IQ).
- **Status (as of 2026-10-12):** Live. Brand IQ / Marketing IQ were launched 12 Nov 2024 as Jasper's "AI knowledge and context layer for marketing" (CMSWire). The trial help article was republished on 2026-09-02.
- **What it does (2-3 sentences):** Jasper generates marketing content (blogs, social, product copy, images) through Canvas, apps, and marketing agents. Brand Voice learns tone and style from examples you upload, and Knowledge, Audiences and Style Guide ground the output. Business tier adds style-guide enforcement, visual guidelines, agents and a no-code app builder.
- **Target customer:** Marketing teams from solo marketers (Pro) to enterprise (Business). The audience types named in the pricing schema include product marketers and PR/comms.
- **Onboarding flow (step by step, as documented):**
  1. Start the 7-day trial from the pricing page. A credit card is required, and you are charged when the trial ends (help centre "Free Trial").
  2. Jasper suggests its self-paced "Jasper Foundations" course as the onboarding path.
  3. Open Jasper IQ, then Brand Voice, and add a voice.
  4. Set visibility (workspace or private).
  5. Upload up to 8 examples: pasted text, .txt/.pdf/.docx files, and/or URLs. Jasper crawls a URL to gather text.
  6. Review the generated description of the voice and optionally turn on excerpts to improve accuracy.
  7. Preview: pick a test format (blog post, LinkedIn post or product description), enter a topic, and generate. Output is shown with and without the voice.
  8. Save. Admins can set a default workspace voice.
  - The help article has 10 steps in total. No time-to-first-output is stated. There is no interview and no social connect. Facts are not cited back to sources. The only check is the side-by-side preview; there is no approval gate.
  - Source: help.jasper.ai Brand Voice article; help.jasper.ai Free Trial article.
- **Pricing:** (checked 2026-10-12, jasper.ai/pricing)
  - **Pro:** $69/seat/mo billed monthly, or $59/seat/mo billed yearly (about 20% off, 12-month commitment). Includes 1 seat, 2 Brand Voices, 5 knowledge assets and 3 Audiences. No Style Guide and no Visual Guidelines.
  - **Business:** custom quote with a minimum 12-month commitment. Unlimited voices, knowledge, audiences and visual guidelines; Style Guide; agents (GEO, translation, deep research); custom agent builder; credits for GEO Hub and agents.
  - **Trial:** 7 days, card required.
- **What it does well (specific, evidence-backed):**
  - Accepts multi-source voice input (text, files and URLs, up to 8) and then shows a with/without preview, which makes the voice feel tangible straight away (help article).
  - The voice is visible as an editable written description, not a black box (help article).
  - Clean governance layering: workspace default voice, private voices, and Business-only Style Guide rules such as word replacements and punctuation.
  - Strong reviewer sentiment: 4.8/5 from 1,855 Capterra reviews. Reviewers cite keeping brand voice consistent across workflows (Capterra).
  - Known weakness, from the same reviews: generic or robotic output and hallucinated facts that need heavy editing. That is a direct opening for JamJam's claims gate (Capterra).
- **What it lacks vs JamJam full build plan:**
  - No guided interview for building a brand from scratch; it assumes the brand already exists (B1).
  - Facts are not evidence-cited back to the source page (B2), and there is no Evidenced/Inferred/Missing coverage map (B3).
  - Voice and knowledge only. No purpose, positioning or origin kit (B4 is partial: Audiences exist, the strategy layer does not).
  - No approval gates on the kit; preview only (B5). No kit versioning or export (B6).
  - No documented no-AI-slop pass or claims gate (R2). Reviewers report hallucinated facts.
  - No native social publishing, reply queue or inbox (R4-R6).
  - No on-chain registration, Verify page or Ledger (P1-P3). No ambassador payouts (Y1-Y3).
- **What JamJam should steal / learn:**
  - Make the with/without voice preview the last step of kit approval: "here is a post without your voice, here it is with it".
  - Let the voice step accept "up to N examples, any mix of URL, file or paste".
  - Show the extracted voice as a readable, editable paragraph before saving.
  - Card-required trials draw complaints. JamJam's email sign-in with no card is a contrast worth stating.
- **Coverage vs loop:** Build: Partly · Run: Partly · Prove: No · Pay: No

### WRITER (https://writer.com)
- **Category:** Enterprise generative-AI platform (WRITER Agent, playbooks, Knowledge Graph RAG) with voice profiles, style guides and terms.
- **Status (as of 2026-10-12):** Live. The plans page now has two tiers, Starter and Enterprise.
- **What it does (2-3 sentences):** WRITER provides an agent and a document editor for enterprise teams. Output is grounded in a graph-based Knowledge Graph and governed by voice profiles, style guides and term lists. Its Knowledge Graph can return inline citations to source files and web pages.
- **Target customer:** Enterprise teams (marketing, comms, ops). Starter is capped at 5 users.
- **Onboarding flow (step by step, as documented):**
  1. Start the 14-day Starter trial. No credit card is required (writer.com/plans).
  2. Go to Customize, then Voice & instructions, then New voice (opens AI Studio).
  3. Paste on-brand examples into up to 8 text boxes: at least 300 words, 500+ recommended, one content type per voice. Or skip and write a description. The article lists no URL or file inputs for voice.
  4. WRITER generates adjectives per example and a voice profile (overview plus characteristics). You can re-extract if it sounds wrong.
  5. Name the voice and describe when to use it, then adjust perspective settings.
  6. Connect a style guide and terms. This is required for them to apply to agent outputs.
  7. Test with sample prompts, then create the voice.
  - Voices can be Personal, Team or Org; only admins set defaults. No interview. No URL scrape for voice. Knowledge Graph citations exist (developer docs) but the voice profile does not cite sources. No approval gate is documented for the voice.
  - Source: support.writer.com voice profile article.
- **Pricing:** (checked 2026-10-12)
  - **Starter:** writer.com/plans does not show a price, only "monthly or annual per-seat plans with fixed credit limits". A third-party pricing guide dated 2026-05-07 reports $39/user/mo monthly or $29/user/mo annual. Treat this as unverified on the vendor page. Up to 5 users, 5 playbooks, 3 routines, 3 connectors, 50 GB Knowledge Graph, one team personality profile.
  - **Enterprise:** contact sales. Unlimited users and free collaborators, departmental brand and voice profiles.
  - **Trial:** 14 days, no card. 20% off for non-profit and education.
- **What it does well (specific, evidence-backed):**
  - Sets a minimum sample size (300 words, 500+ recommended) and suggests one content type per voice. That is honest about input quality (support article).
  - Knowledge Graph supports inline citations that link each claim to a file or web snippet. This is the closest thing in the segment to evidence-cited output (dev.writer.com).
  - A grounding-level control sets how closely answers must match source material (dev.writer.com).
  - Clear hierarchy of personal, team and org voices, with admin-set defaults.
- **What it lacks vs JamJam full build plan:**
  - No brand-from-scratch interview (B1). Voice comes from pasted samples only, with no URL scrape.
  - Citations live in Knowledge Graph Q&A, not in a brand kit. There is no "keep the fact only if the quote is on the page" rule and no coverage map (B2, B3).
  - No purpose, positioning or origin kit (B4). No kit approval gates (B5) or kit export/versioning (B6).
  - No social publishing, reply queue or inbox (R4-R6). No per-draft claims gate is documented (R2).
  - Prove and Pay are absent (P1-P3, Y1-Y3).
- **What JamJam should steal / learn:**
  - Show a minimum-evidence threshold in the UI, for example "need 300+ words of your own writing".
  - Expose a grounding level, or a "strict evidence" toggle, as a user-facing control.
  - Use WRITER's citation format (source name plus snippet plus id) as a reference for how JamJam shows the supporting quote next to each kit fact.
  - No-card 14-day trial.
- **Coverage vs loop:** Build: Partly · Run: Partly · Prove: No · Pay: No

### Copy.ai (https://www.copy.ai), now owned by Fullcast
- **Category:** GTM AI platform (chat plus workflows) with Brand Voice and Infobase modules.
- **Status (as of 2026-10-12):** Live, but acquired by Fullcast. Copy.ai's own announcement page exists. A third-party tracker dates the deal to 2025-10-15. Help docs are now hosted at support.fullcast.com. The free plan has been removed (sunset per the Copy.ai help centre, Feb 2026; removal confirmed by PricePeek on 2026-09-27).
- **What it does (2-3 sentences):** Copy.ai is a chat and workflow platform for sales and marketing (prospecting, content, deal coaching). It is being folded into Fullcast's RevOps platform as "Fullcast Propel (powered by Copy.ai)". Brand Voice and Infobase keep outputs on-brand and factually grounded.
- **Target customer:** Revenue and GTM teams. Workflow tiers are sized at 75-200 seats.
- **Onboarding flow (step by step, as documented):**
  1. Sign up. DealHaul tested this on 2026-09-12: after setup questions, the new account went straight to a card checkout for the full first month, with no trial offered. Copy.ai's terms say a trial may be offered to qualifying users.
  2. Go to Configuration, then Brand Voice, then Create New Brand Voice.
  3. Give one input: a webpage URL, which Copy.ai scrapes and analyses, or a text sample.
  4. Name and save the voice. Voices are unlimited, and you select one per chat.
  5. Optionally, go to Configuration, then Infobase, then Create. Upload TXT/PDF or paste text, then reference it in chat with "#". The full entry is injected into the prompt.
  - Roughly 4 clicks to a voice. No interview. No citation of facts. No approval gate on the voice.
  - Source: support.fullcast.com Copy.ai docs.
- **Pricing:** (checked 2026-10-12, copy.ai/prices)
  - **Chat:** $29/mo monthly or $24/mo annual ($288/yr). 5 seats, unlimited chat words.
  - **Growth:** $1,000/mo (annual, $12k/yr). 75 seats, 20K workflow credits.
  - **Expansion:** $2,000/mo (annual). 150 seats, 45K credits.
  - **Scale:** $3,000/mo (annual). 200 seats, 75K credits.
  - **Enterprise:** custom.
  - DealHaul also found an in-app-only "Agents" plan at $249/mo ($211/mo yearly) that the public page does not show. That figure comes from a third party and is not on the vendor pricing page.
  - No free plan. A trial is not guaranteed. Card required at checkout.
- **What it does well (specific, evidence-backed):**
  - Fastest voice setup in the segment: one URL or one sample, analyse, name, done (Fullcast docs).
  - Unlimited voices that you switch per chat, which suits agencies.
  - Infobase is a simple, explicit "facts store" the user controls, and data is siloed per workspace (Fullcast docs).
- **What it lacks vs JamJam full build plan:**
  - No interview, no brand strategy kit and no coverage map (B1, B3, B4).
  - URL scraping yields a voice, not cited facts (B2). Infobase facts are pasted by the user, not verified against a source.
  - No approval gates or versioning on brand assets (B5, B6). No claims gate or anti-slop pass (R2).
  - No social publishing, inbox or reply queue (R4-R6). No Prove or Pay (P1-P3, Y1-Y3).
  - Strategic drift toward RevOps makes it less relevant as a brand tool over time.
- **What JamJam should steal / learn:**
  - Copy the "#" mention pattern: let users pull a specific kit section or evidenced fact into a draft prompt inline.
  - Offer one-URL voice creation as the express path, with the 6-step interview as the deepening path.
  - The free-plan removal and surprise card checkout show what not to do for a self-serve brand tool.
- **Coverage vs loop:** Build: Partly · Run: Partly · Prove: No · Pay: No

### Typeface (https://www.typeface.ai)
- **Category:** Enterprise agentic marketing orchestration (Arc Graph brand intelligence, Arc Fabric agents, Arc Loop performance feedback).
- **Status (as of 2026-10-12):** Live and enterprise-only. Arc Graph, Fabric and Loop shipped as one "Orchestration Engine" on 2026-09-22. No acquisition found; Salesforce Ventures is an investor. The /pricing page returns not-found (fetch failed on 2026-10-12, and a tracker reported a 404 in June 2026).
- **What it does (2-3 sentences):** Arc Graph ingests brand books, style guides and writing samples and turns them into structured, enforceable rules and multiple voices. Those rules are also exposed to Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini. Agents create content against the rules, and Arc Loop feeds campaign results back. The system ranks variants and requires human approval before anything publishes.
- **Target customer:** Large enterprises and house-of-brands organisations. Sales-led, with SI partners (Cognizant, Firstsource).
- **Onboarding flow (step by step, as documented):**
  1. Book a demo; there is no self-serve sign-up (homepage CTAs are demo, tour, or talk to the team).
  2. Bring source material: brand books, style guides and writing samples, uploaded or pulled from Google Drive or SharePoint. A website URL is not listed as an input.
  3. Brand Graph extracts copy and visual guidelines into a structured brand kit, with separate voices per channel.
  4. The agent guides refinement and asks for input only when needed.
  5. Brand linting flags contradictions, duplicates and broken references before go-live.
  6. For house-of-brands setups, the team approves overrides, removals and additions one rule at a time.
  7. Real-time enforcement runs during creation. Each fired rule is named with its reason.
  - A third-party tracker reports sales-led onboarding of 6-16 weeks (unverified on the vendor site). No trial.
  - Source: typeface.ai/product/brand-agent; usagepricing.com.
- **Pricing:** Unverified, because Typeface publishes no rate card and its pricing page is gone. A third-party tracker (usagepricing.com) reports indicative Team at about $49/user/mo, Business at about $99/user/mo, and enterprise Arc contracts of roughly $100K to $1M+/yr. None of these are vendor-confirmed. No free tier or trial.
- **What it does well (specific, evidence-backed):**
  - Per-rule approval of extracted brand rules. This is the closest analogue in the segment to JamJam's approval gates (Brand Agent page).
  - Brand linting before use: contradictions, duplicates, broken references (Brand Agent page).
  - Explainable enforcement: when a rule fires, it shows which rule and why (Brand Agent page; PPC Land).
  - Human approval required before Arc Loop variants publish (PPC Land, 2026-09-25).
  - Exports brand rules into the assistants staff already use (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini via MCP).
- **What it lacks vs JamJam full build plan:**
  - Assumes existing brand books. No from-scratch interview (B1) and no website-evidence ingestion with quotes (B2).
  - No visible Evidenced/Inferred/Missing coverage map (B3), though linting is adjacent.
  - Approvals are per rule, but the who/when/why record is not documented as an audit trail (B5).
  - Enterprise-only and inaccessible to small or crypto brands (business-model gap).
  - No public verification of official content (P1-P3). No ambassador payouts (Y1-Y3). No inbox or reply queue (R5, R6).
- **What JamJam should steal / learn:**
  - Kit linting: before Gate 3, run a check for contradictions between positioning, voice and messaging, and show the issues.
  - Show "rule fired: X, because Y" on every draft check in the claims gate and voice-fit score.
  - Offer an MCP export of the approved kit so the brand's own Claude or ChatGPT uses it (complements the aDNA zip, B6).
- **Coverage vs loop:** Build: Partly · Run: Yes · Prove: No · Pay: No

### Google Pomelli (https://labs.google.com/pomelli)
- **Category:** Free Google Labs AI marketing tool for SMBs: Business DNA, campaigns, brand books and websites.
- **Status (as of 2026-10-12):** Live public beta (Google Labs). It launched 2025-10-28 in the US, CA, AU and NZ. Pomelli Agent, Brand Books and Websites were added on 2026-05-19 (Google I/O). Coverage of geography conflicts: Gadgetbond (May 2026) lists only US/CA/AU/NZ, while a secondary blog claims the UK, EU and "170+ markets".
- **What it does (2-3 sentences):** Pomelli builds a "Business DNA" profile (tone of voice, fonts, colours, images) from your website, or since May 2026 from uploaded docs and photos or a chat with the Pomelli Agent if you are starting from scratch. It then generates campaign ideas and on-brand social and ad assets. It can also produce a shareable brand book and a hosted website.
- **Target customer:** Small and medium businesses without a design team, including businesses with no web presence.
- **Onboarding flow (step by step, as documented):**
  1. Sign in with a Google account. No card.
  2. Build Business DNA, by one of three routes: (a) enter your website URL, and Pomelli analyses the site and its images; (b) upload product docs and photos; (c) chat with the Pomelli Agent to define the brand from scratch. A secondary source says the agent asks about mission, audience and values, but the exact questions are unverified.
  3. Review and correct the DNA, including natural-language edits such as "more formal tone" (secondary source).
  4. Generate campaign ideas, picking one or typing a prompt.
  5. Edit the generated assets in the tool and download them.
  6. Optionally generate a brand book in one click, and a website in a few clicks.
  - Google calls the original flow three steps. No time-to-first-output is stated. Facts are not cited. There is no approval gate; you just download.
  - Source: Google blog (Oct 2025, May 2026).
- **Pricing:** Free during the beta (Gadgetbond 2026-05-20; Google's Oct 2025 post does not mention price). No post-beta pricing has been announced. No card.
- **What it does well (specific, evidence-backed):**
  - Three entry routes to the same profile (URL, uploads, conversation). This handles both existing and from-scratch brands (Google blog, May 2026).
  - Very short path to value: URL, then DNA, then campaign ideas, then downloadable assets (Google blog, Oct 2025).
  - Converts the brand profile directly into artifacts people share: a brand book PDF and a hosted website (Google blog, May 2026).
  - Free with Google distribution. Google says "millions" of assets have been made.
- **What it lacks vs JamJam full build plan:**
  - The Business DNA is visual plus tone. No evidence-cited facts (B2) and no coverage map (B3).
  - No strategic kit sections such as purpose, positioning or origin. Mission and audience are asked about but not structured as a kit (B4 partial).
  - No approval gates or audit record (B5). No kit versioning (B6).
  - No anti-slop or claims gate (R2). No direct publishing; download only (R4). No inbox, replies or analytics (R5-R9).
  - No Prove or Pay (P1-P3, Y1-Y3). Beta with no SLA, and Google can withdraw Labs products.
- **What JamJam should steal / learn:**
  - Offer the three-door start (URL, upload, chat) on one screen, with every door leading to the same kit.
  - Produce a one-click shareable brand book from the approved kit. It is a tangible artifact to show investors and partners, and it would pair well with the on-chain hash ("this brand book is kit v1, verified").
  - Natural-language corrections on the kit ("make the voice more formal") instead of form edits.
  - Positioning: Pomelli is free and good enough for visuals. JamJam should not compete on visuals; it should compete on evidence, approvals and proof.
- **Coverage vs loop:** Build: Yes · Run: Partly · Prove: No · Pay: No

### Canva (https://www.canva.com): Brand Kit, Brand Kit Builder, brand voice
- **Category:** Design platform with Brand Kits, an AI Brand Kit Builder (from URL or PDF), brand voice for Magic Write, and design approvals.
- **Status (as of 2026-10-12):** Live.
- **What it does (2-3 sentences):** Brand Kit Builder auto-creates a Brand Kit (logos, colours, fonts, graphics, photography, brand voice and guidelines) from a website URL or a brand-guidelines PDF. Brand voice is a 500-character text field that Magic Write applies to generated copy. Teams and Enterprise can require design approval before anyone can publish or download.
- **Target customer:** Individuals to enterprises. The brand features are mostly Pro, Teams/Business and Enterprise.
- **Onboarding flow (step by step, as documented):**
  1. Start a Pro or Business free trial. Help centre: the duration shown at sign-up applies, and a trial started without payment details ends with no charge. A Canva Verified Expert says trials were standardised at 30 days in 2024 (secondary).
  2. Open the Brand tab on the homepage, then a new or empty Brand Kit, then Brand Kit Builder. It can also run during team onboarding.
  3. Enter a website URL (analysis is "usually instant") or upload a brand PDF under 200 MB (about 1 minute).
  4. Review the extracted logos, colours, fonts, graphics, photos, brand voice and guidelines. Canva advises reviewing everything.
  5. Edit or add elements, then save.
  6. Optionally edit brand voice (max 500 characters of free text) and use it through Magic Write ("Generate in this brand voice").
  7. Optionally turn on Brand Controls, then Design approvals: set submitters and reviewers and require approval to publish.
  - About 5 steps to a kit; Canva calls website extraction near-instant. There are no cited facts. Approval applies to designs, not to the kit.
  - Source: canva.com/help/brand-kit-builder, /brand-voice, /design-approval.
- **Pricing:** (checked 2026-10-12, canva.com/pricing)
  - **Free:** $0, 1 Brand Kit (3 colours only).

## Segment takeaways
- Onboarding has converged on "URL in, kit out". Pomelli, Canva Brand Kit Builder, Blaze and Copy.ai all build a brand profile from a website in seconds to minutes. Canva and Pomelli do it for free or at Pro-plan prices, so a visual or voice kit from a URL is now a commodity.
- No one cites kit facts back to a quote on the source page. WRITER's Knowledge Graph has inline citations and Frontify's assistant links to guideline sections, but no tool keeps a brand fact only when its supporting quote exists, and none shows Evidenced/Inferred/Missing coverage. B2 and B3 are genuinely open space.
- From-scratch brand building arrived in 2026. Pomelli Agent (May 2026) and Branding5 build from conversation or questions. Branding5's AI pre-fill of interview answers from the website is the nearest pattern to JamJam B1+B3, but it has no evidence display.
- Approval gates are weak or optional in self-serve tools. Jasper and WRITER only offer a preview, and Blaze's approvals are optional under "Autopilot". Canva enforces approval on designs, not on the brand kit. Only enterprise Typeface approves extracted rules one at a time and requires human approval before publishing. JamJam's mandatory three-gate kit approval with a who/when/why record is distinctive below enterprise tier.
- Nobody in the segment does public provenance or verification of official content, or creator payouts. Prove and Pay are uncontested here.
- Pricing anchors: voice tools at $24-$69/seat/mo (Copy.ai Chat, Jasper Pro, WRITER Starter reported $29-$39). SMB URL-to-publishing at $79-$149/mo (Blaze). A one-off strategy kit at $147 (Branding5). Enterprise is custom or six figures (Typeface). Card-free trials (WRITER, Blaze, Canva, Pomelli) are the norm; Jasper and Copy.ai require a card.
- Market churn in 2026: Copy.ai was absorbed into Fullcast RevOps and dropped its free plan, BrandWell pivoted away from brand voice, Blaze retired Copilot for Autopilot, and Typeface went enterprise-only with no public pricing.
- UX patterns worth adopting: a with/without voice preview (Jasper); confirm-screen wizards and trait chips for voice (Blaze); PDF guideline ingest plus time estimates (Canva); "rule fired, because" explanations and kit linting (Typeface); a three-door start of URL, upload or chat, plus a one-click brand book (Pomelli).
